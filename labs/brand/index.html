The brand

Grounded, exact, and easy to trust.

McCracken Land Services is an independent Oklahoma landman practice. The brand should feel like the work: careful, plainspoken, and dependable. Deep navy carries the weight and steadiness of the field, and a single gold accent marks the details that matter. Nothing flashy, nothing that gets in the way of the information.

Story

Where the practice comes from.

McCracken Land Services was built on a simple idea: that careful, personal land work is worth more than a fast answer. Michael McCracken has spent his career in the field and in the courthouse, running title, tracing ownership, and putting deals on solid legal footing. The brand carries that history. It is not a large firm dressed up to look bigger than it is, and it does not try to be. It is one experienced landman doing the work himself, and standing behind every decimal.

Everything the brand touches should reinforce that promise. The look is steady and unadorned, the writing is plain, and the details are exact, because that is exactly how the work is done.

Personality

How the brand carries itself.

Four traits shape every choice, from a headline to a color. When something feels off, it usually means it has drifted from one of these.

Grounded

Rooted in the field and the record. Confident without posturing, and never louder than the work.

Exact

Precise to the decimal. Careful with words and numbers, because in this work a small error is expensive.

Plainspoken

Clear over clever. Says what it means in the fewest honest words, the way a landman talks to a client.

Dependable

Steady and consistent. Shows up the same way every time, so people know what they are getting.

Logo

The mark

The primary logo is the wordmark with the emblem, in white and gold. It is built for dark backgrounds and is the default across the site, documents, and headers. Where color cannot be used, a single-color black or white version is fine.

Primary, white and gold on navy

One-color black, for light backgrounds and single-color printing, stamps, or faxes

  • Place the full-color logo on navy or another dark background.
  • On light backgrounds, or where only one ink is available, use the solid black version.
  • Black and white is fine, the mark is designed to hold up in a single color.
  • Keep clear space around it equal to the height of the emblem.
  • Scale it proportionally, never stretch or squeeze.
  • Do not recolor the mark into anything outside navy, gold, black, or white.
  • Do not add shadows, outlines, or effects.
  • Do not place it on a busy photo without a dark overlay.

What the mark means

The emblem is drawn from the tools of the trade. The angled lines and arrows read as GIS and mapping linework, the directional and section lines a landman works from, and the symbol above the N is the gas well symbol used across GIS and mapping systems. It keeps the mark tied to the actual work: land, maps, and minerals.

Clear space

Give the mark room

Keep a margin around the logo at least equal to the height of the emblem. Nothing else, no text, rule, or edge, sits inside that space.

Minimum size

Keep it legible

Web • 160px wide min
Print • 1 in wide min

Below these sizes the wordmark starts to break down. When space is that tight, use the emblem on its own instead.

Iconography

Icons and linework

Icons are drawn in one consistent style so they read as a set: a single line weight, rounded ends and corners, and no fills. They echo the linework in the emblem, the same maps-and-measurements language the brand is built on.

Weight. Draw on a 24-unit grid at a 1.6 stroke. Keep the weight even across a set.

Corners. Round the caps and joins. No sharp points, no hairlines.

Fills. Outline only. Color the stroke navy on light, white or gold on dark.

Restraint. One icon per idea. If a label is clearer than a picture, use the label.

Imagery

Photography

Photography is real and from the field: rigs, pumpjacks, section lines, and the country the work happens in, shot at golden hour or dusk for warm, low light. Over a photo, lay a navy gradient so white text stays readable and the image never competes with the message. Keep it honest. No stock handshakes, no glossy corporate staging.

Messaging

Tagline and messaging

A short, consistent set of lines does the heavy lifting. Lead with the tagline, support it with the positioning line, and keep the boilerplate identical everywhere it appears.

Primary tagline

Know your land.
Lease with confidence.

Used on the hero and in headers. It states the promise in the client's terms: clarity first, then the confidence to act on it.

Secondary line

Rooted in the land. Driven by the details.

A quieter line for section bands and print, where the tagline would repeat.

One-line positioning

An independent Oklahoma landman practice handling title, ownership research, leasing, and due diligence for oil, gas, and renewables.

Boilerplate

McCracken Land Services is an independent land services firm led by landman Michael McCracken, handling the research, title, and field work that energy projects run on. Every job is handled personally, with the accuracy and follow-through that keep projects moving.

Voice

How it sounds

The writing should sound like a landman who knows the work, talking plainly to a client or a broker. Confident, specific, and free of filler.

Do

  • Write plainly and get to the point.
  • Be specific about the work and the result.
  • Use "we," led personally by Michael McCracken.
  • Vary sentence length; keep it human.

Avoid

  • Hype, buzzwords, and empty superlatives.
  • Stiff corporate phrasing.
  • Em dashes; use commas or periods instead.
  • Tidy rule-of-three lists that read as filler.
